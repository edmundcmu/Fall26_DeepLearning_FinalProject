### 5.1 Data audit and UMA-M transfer-baseline preparation

We acquired a versioned Cu/H reference database from the public repository accompanying the reactive hydrogen–copper MLIP research of Stark et al. [1]. These are DFT energy and force labels associated with the MACE benchmark lineage, not predictions generated by MACE. The downloaded export contains 4,220 configurations: 2,535 Cu54H2 structures and 1,685 clean Cu54 slabs. This count differs from the 4,230 configurations described in the original paper; we therefore identify our experiment by the exact repository revision and file checksum rather than claiming an identical dataset version. Three records were removed after confirming identical structures, energies and forces, leaving 4,217 unique configurations.

The file contains no single-H configurations. Accordingly, this stage evaluates **Cu/H energy-and-force transfer**, not isolated-H diffusion on Cu(111). The literature reference method is SRP48, comprising 52% PBE and 48% RPBE, evaluated with FHI-aims [1]. We retain the supplied export's energy convention without reconstructing undocumented atomic-energy offsets. Positions, periodic cells, energies and Cartesian forces are preserved in Å, eV and eV/Å. No stress labels or per-frame mobility constraints are supplied.

Because trajectory and parent-structure identifiers are absent, we predeclared a simulation-cell-family-held-out pilot. Four cell families are identified after rounding cell entries to 10^-6 Å. Two rectangular families form the training pool, the shortest-a rectangular family supplies validation, and the oblique in-plane family is held out for testing. Composition-stratified subsampling with seed 20261023 gives the following **executed** split. No cell family or detected exact duplicate crosses a split. This protocol measures cell-family transfer; it is not a reproduction of the published random-split benchmark and does not establish absence of pretraining overlap.

| Split | Configurations | Cu54H2 | Cu54 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Training | 500 | 296 | 204 |
| Validation | 100 | 64 | 36 |
| Locked test | 200 | 116 | 84 |

The selected backbone is **UMA-M-1.1 (`uma-m-1p1`)**, conditioned on `oc20` [2]. Since its pretrained catalyst reference differs from SRP48, evaluation against this database tests reference-method adaptation as well as domain transfer. The starter implements two model conditions: the untouched pretrained model (U0) and the pinned FAIRChem native transfer procedure. The latter initializes a new conservative energy/force head and updates the backbone; it is not head-preserving fine-tuning or adapter selection. The planned pilot uses AdamW, batch size 1, learning rate 10^-4, weight decay 10^-3, and first two, then 2,000 optimizer updates. These are planned settings, not completed runs.

For this initial native-upstream baseline, the training objective is the upstream normalized per-atom energy MAE and per-atom force-vector L2 error, with coefficients 20 and 2. It **differs from the species-balanced MSE in Section 3.3**; subsequent adapter comparisons must use one shared objective across adapted methods. Normalization and reference terms use training data only. Evaluation separately reports H- and Cu-component force errors, configuration-energy errors, and optional energy errors aligned by training-only composition offsets. All supplied force labels are scored because movable-atom metadata are unavailable.

As an executed numerical sanity reference, a predictor using zero forces and the training-set mean energy for each composition yields the following validation errors. It is deliberately nonphysical and is **not a UMA or MACE result**.

| Evaluation condition | H force MAE (eV/Å) | Cu force MAE (eV/Å) | Energy MAE (eV/configuration) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Zero force / training-composition-mean energy | 1.091511 | 0.305009 | 4.049624 |
| Pretrained UMA-M, U0 | Not run | Not run | Not run |
| Native UMA-M full-backbone transfer | Not run | Not run | Not run |

The data conversion checks, split/metric tests, and Python syntax checks have been executed on CPU. UMA-M weights require approved Hugging Face access, and CUDA training has not been run in the current environment. Thus no UMA-M physical MAE, improvement percentage, GPU time, or convergence result is claimed. The next evidence required is a successful two-update gradient/checkpoint test, validation inference from the saved checkpoint, and fixed-protocol training followed by locked-test evaluation. This ordinary transfer baseline does not satisfy the draft's separate Force-Gradient adapter-reproduction requirement, which is deferred at this stage. No diffusion barrier or MD result is reported.
